Syllabus

Course Overview

This course introduces students to the main ways that sociologists conduct research. Key themes include the different approaches that researchers use to collect and analyze data about society: asking people questions (interviews and surveys), observing and participating in social life (ethnography and experiments), and examining records and documents (contemporary and historical). We will consider the strengths and weaknesses of these methods in order to see how each approach is appropriate for some questions and less useful for others. Throughout the term, students will also gain firsthand experience with social research by conducting their own research project on a topic of their choice and producing a small-scale research paper.

Section Goals and Structure

The main goals of the discussion sections are to 1). apply course material to designing and conducting a research project, 2). provide guidance as you develop your own research project and paper, and 3). discuss key methodological concepts and techniques. The discussion section provides a space for you to ask questions about course materials, learn from your peers via groups work, and participate in collective learning.

To facilitate these goals, there will be workshopping weeks and lecturing weeks. In the workshopping weeks, you will spend most of the time working in groups to complete the upcoming assignment(s), which will serve as an important part of your final paper. I will be there to answer your questions and give a helping hand when you struggle. In the lecturing weeks, we will go through the major theme(s) covered in the week and focus on concepts and/or techniques on which you still have confusions, aiming to increase the clarity. I will organize class activities, if necessary, for these weeks.

Another Survey Question Example

Oct. 2, 13:56 ET

Dear Students, 

We did not have enough time to look at another survey question today in section 004 but I did for section 005, so I'd like to share that with you via this announcement. 

We can also look at income - in [GSS 2006](https://gss.norc.org/content/dam/gss/get-documentation/pdf/quex/2006%20GSS%20V1.pdf), there are at least two kinds of questions asked. 
One is Q52 (search FINRELA), "Compared with American families in general, would you say your family income is – far below average, below average, average, above average, or far above average?" This is on one of the subjective dimensions of income, which may provide you some more information. This is also a good example for probe in surveys, as it lists: "(PROBE: Just your best guess.)"
The other is Q108 (search INCOME06), "In which of these groups did your total family income, from all sources, fall last year – 2005 – before taxes, that is. Just tell me the letter." And the choices are a series of intervals. This is on the objective dimension of income, collecting information as a categorical variable. Some surveys also ask for specific numbers, such as in China's GSS (CGSS). 

In addition to serve as a sample to which you may refer for your own surveys, I think GSS 2026's questions on income also reflects that there can be multiple questions for one variable / dimension of a variable / factor of an index. Of course, this depends on your purpose and RQ. However, don't limit yourself to preparing just one question for one variable of yours. 

Hope this may be helpful.

Proper Formats are very Important

Sept. 24, 13:50 ET

Dear Students, 

As I'm grading your Assignment #2a, a major issue for many of you is the formatting - your main body texts, headings, and/or the bibliography page. These problems are quite obvious, as formatting errors usually stand out first when I open a document, especially when I only need to skim through your submissions rather than look into details. I have to emphasize again that proper formats are very important, not only for this class but also, in fact, all your future writing and professional developments. If your documents look beautiful, you will leave your readers a good first impression; on the other hand, your audiences will be annoyed if there are formatting errors.

Although I did not take points off this time for a complete/incomplete assignment, to highly encourage you double check the formats before each submission, there will be point deduction for future assignments, and I'll be strict on this. We are a ULWR class, so writing quality does matter. As stated in our Final Paper rubric (p. 4), you need to have your bibliography present and "following a correct format" to be in the exceptional category, which is full mark for overall quality (~15% of total grade); if there are formatting errors, you'll drop to the satisfactory category, which is associated with some points off. I believe that, compared to other things on which you'll be graded, it's relatively the easiest to get full mark on formatting - so please check it and keep all of the points. 

I noticed that many of you are directly copying and pasting texts from prior assignments, which is totally fine and is what you need do as long as you revise based on our comments and feedback. If you keep the same formatting mistakes, then you're not revising them. In addition, on one hand, note that the copying and pasting may sometimes change your texts to the original, plain format, such that you need to bold and/or italicize them again; on the other hand, if you make them all correct this time, it saves your time next time, doesn't it?

Again, please do check your formatting each time before you submit your essays. It includes but is not limited to
1. consistent font, font size, and space between paragraphs throughout the essay
2. the words "Works Cited" or "References" present at the beginning, bolded, and center-aligned for your bibliography page
3. each citation using hanging indent
4. journal titles italicized and not abbreviated 

For APA format, you may also refer to [Purdue OWL](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/reference_list_basic_rules.html), whose link is also updated in section wk #2 slides, in addition to our library guide. For ASA format, I've already shared with you [the quick tip](https://www.asanet.org/wp-content/uploads/savvy/documents/teaching/pdfs/Quick_Tips_for_ASA_Style.pdf). To ensure that all of you know the relevant techniques, I'll go over the steps in both Microsoft Word and Google Docs tomorrow.

Confounders and Assignment #2a

Sept. 18, 14:52 ET

Dear Students, 

I may have not made it 100% clear, so I'd like to clarify / add something for our conversations on confounders in today's sections.
1. If my graphs/examples confused you, ignore them and rely on those Prof. Boutyline offered in lectures.
2. If this is helpful, my co-GSI Alvaro offered a definition on confounders as "variables that create a back-door path between the independent variable and the dependent variable, giving rise to alternative explanations or spurious correlations between variables" (Huntington-Klein 2022). This aligns with the idea of asking "what else?" when looking for potential confounders. 
3. In your Assignment #2a (and #2b, of course), confounders are the main characters of your counterarguments, and counterarguments can be something like, "Well, there can be another way in which it happens." For my origin (IV) -> destination (DV) example in class, if my thesis / main argument is "origin -> education -> destination" (i.e., education as mediator), my counterargument could question this by saying, "Is there another path? Perhaps! Maybe origin -> first job -> destination?" or something like that. In this case, "first job" is my confounder. 
4. Put all these together, I think the key is to find alternative mechanism(s) behind your causal claim, other than your main hypothesis. 

We can come back to confounders and spend some more time next week when you work on Assignment #2b. 

Also, regarding citations - while I continue to encourage you cite whenever prior studies help you and/or your arguments can be more convincing via citing, I don't think you have to cite everything this time. If some variables (and the rationales / logics behind) are very straightforward, it's ok to just go ahead and use them. 

Good luck and see you next week!
